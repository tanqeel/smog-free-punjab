Working tool 1 · Health Guard

Same air, different advice — pick a city and a person

This is the decision IQAir and the official dashboard don't give you. Advice follows WHO thresholds and Punjab EPA advisories — it is guidance, not a medical diagnosis. If someone is struggling to breathe, seek medical help, don't wait for a website.

Thresholds: WHO 2021 guidelines (PM2.5 5 µg/m³ annual / 15 µg/m³ 24-hour) · Singapore MOH mask guidance · Punjab EPA advisory. Full sources at the foot of this page.

Masks — what actually works

N95 / FFP2 / KN95, well-fitted: ≥95% efficient against fine particles. Surgical and cloth masks do NOT filter PM2.5 — “normal surgical masks are not effective in filtering fine particles (2.5 µm or less).” N95 does not stop gases, and at AQI 400–500 staying indoors matters more than any mask. For children, no certified mask standard was reported — in hazardous air, staying indoors is the best protection.

Sources: Singapore Ministry of Health FAQ; CNA Lifestyle; Indian Express (Delhi doctors)

Myth: “Plants clean my room” — False

NASA's 1989 study was in small sealed chambers. In a real room, normal air exchange removes pollutants far faster than plants do. The US EPA states there is currently no evidence that a reasonable number of houseplants removes significant quantities of pollutants — you would need roughly 10 plants per square foot to compete with ventilation.

Sources: US EPA via Iowa State Extension; Drexel University review of 12 studies

DIY purifier that is proven

The Corsi–Rosenthal box — a box fan + four MERV-13 filters + cardboard + tape — cut classroom PM2.5 by over 70% in one study and by an average of 61% across 59 class sessions in a 2026 ACS study, at a fraction of a HEPA unit's cost. It cleans one room — it does not solve smog, and fan safety and filter availability in Punjab must be checked.

Source: ACS Environmental Science & Technology / ES&T Air, Aug 2026

Working tool 2 · The AI core

Tomorrow's forecast — a real model, honestly tested

Yesterday's AQI + weather beats a fancy neural net trained badly. A 2026 multi-horizon study found the most recent PM2.5 value carried 47.8% of predictive power, simple linear regression won at 1 hour (RMSE 10.68), and gradient-boosted trees won at 24 hours (RMSE 24.26, R² 0.467) — while LSTM underperformed at 24 hours. That is why this project builds trees first.

The model Fiza builds

  • Data: hourly PM2.5/AQI history + hourly weather (temperature, humidity, wind speed & direction, rain) for Lahore, Multan, Gujranwala, Faisalabad — Open-Meteo archive + OpenAQ ground truth.
  • Features: lag-1, lag-24, lag-48 AQI; 24-hour rolling mean/max; hour, weekday, month; wind as sin/cos; rain in last 6/24 hours; optional NASA FIRMS fire count (Phase 2).
  • Models, in order: (1) Persistence baseline — “tomorrow = today”; (2) Ridge regression; (3) Random Forest (100–300 trees) — the recommended first model; (4) Gradient Boosting; (5) LSTM only if time remains.
  • Public target: beat persistence by ≥15% on MAE, and get the AQI band right ≥75% of the time on a held-out November week. If it misses, report the miss and why.

Methods: scikit-learn time-series practice (never a random split — it leaks the future); Research Square multi-horizon comparison, Jan 2026

Evaluation — non-negotiable rules

  • Train on earlier data, test on the most recent 2–4 weeks — ideally a smog week the model never saw.
  • Report MAE and RMSE (µg/m³ or AQI points), R², and AQI-band accuracy — plus the persistence baseline's same numbers.
  • Never quote a single “% accuracy” without its baseline, test set and metric. Published 24-hour ranges are roughly RMSE 12–25 — a claimed “99%” collapses under one judge question.
  • The model cannot predict a sudden crackdown, a Diwali fire spike, or street-level air from a 45 km model grid. The site labels every forecast: “Forecast, not a measurement.”
Honest status: Smog Free Punjab's own forecast accuracy cannot be stated until the model is trained and evaluated on its held-out week. This page gives the method and the target — not an invented number.

Working tool 3 · SkySnap

One sky photo → an estimate, shown honestly

Delhi students won the Marconi Society Celestini top prize ($1,500) for Air Cognizer — an app estimating AQI from a sky photo, with a claimed error under 5% in their test conditions. SkySnap copies the honesty, not just the trick: your photo's estimate is always shown beside the station value. Try it — pick any outdoor photo; the page measures its brightness, blueness and haze in your browser. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

How it works here: the browser samples the top half of your photo (the sky), measures blue-vs-grey balance, brightness and contrast, and maps them to a rough AQI range. Clouds, time of day, white balance and phone model all shift the result — that is the pitfall, stated up front.

The lid, not the smoke — inversion

In winter, a temperature inversion locks cold air at the surface “like a lid, with warm air trapping pollutants near the surface,” hemmed in by the Himalayas and the Vindhya Range. That is why the forecast must include wind, humidity and rain — in Nov 2024, wind and rain, not the construction ban, cleared Lahore's air.

Sources: NASA Earth Observatory; Pakistan Journal of Science, Dec 2024 (Green Lockdown study: no significant localised AQI improvement from the ban)

Enforcement is real — and not enough alone

1 Jan – 18 Nov 2026: 84,370 brick-kiln inspections, 1,429 kilns demolished, 968 sealed, 1,248 FIRs, Rs 178 million in fines. About 10,394 kilns were counted province-wide; officials say ~8,000 have moved to zigzag technology. Open waste burning was banned province-wide on 16 Oct 2026 — report it on 1373.

Source: Dawn, 21 Nov 2026; EPD Punjab

Fires — label the geography

Pakistani Punjab satellite anomalies: 643 by 10 Nov 2026 vs 817 in the same period last year. Indian Punjab's official count to 8 Nov was 4,459 incidents — a different Punjab. Every fire number must name its Punjab, its satellite, and say “detections, not confirmed fires.”

Ten datasets — what each must contain

  • D1 Hourly AQI/PM2.5 history, 4 cities — datetime, station, lat/lon, PM2.5, PM10, AQI, source (OpenAQ / WAQI / Open-Meteo archive). Minimum 3 months hourly.
  • D2 Hourly weather — temperature, humidity, wind speed/direction, rain, pressure (Open-Meteo).
  • D3 Live AQI cache — refreshed hourly into one JSON file, never called per visitor.
  • D4 Forecast output — 48 hourly steps per city, regenerated daily, with model version.
  • D5 FIRMS fires — lat/lon, date/time UTC, satellite, confidence, FRP. Last 7–30 days, Punjab bounding box.
  • D6 Station registry — one row per station shown, re-checked before the demo.
  • D7 Labelled sky photos — 50–100, with AQI at that hour; split by day.
  • D8 Health content — 7 bands × 5 profiles = 35 advice rows, each ≤25 words, each cited.
  • D9 School data — start with one school done perfectly, real timings.
  • D10 Held-out evaluation week — carved out before training. Sacred: never train on it.

Judge: “Isn't this just IQAir?”

“IQAir shows today's AQI. We show tomorrow's AQI, for your health profile, for your school at 8:45, in Urdu, plus reporting to 1373. IQAir is a source we credit — we add the decision.”

Judge: “What is your accuracy?”

Answer only with your real held-out numbers: MAE, RMSE, band accuracy, and the persistence baseline beside them — and name the week it got worse (e.g. Diwali) and why. Never say “99%” without a test set, metric and baseline.

Judge: “Why Random Forest, not deep learning?”

“At 24 hours, a 2026 comparison found gradient-boosted trees beat LSTM (GBDT RMSE 24.3, R² 0.47), and the last AQI value carried 48% of the signal. We tested both and show both — the forest won on our data.”

Judge: “Do plants or surgical masks help?”

“Plants do not clean a room — the EPA says no reasonable number removes significant pollutants. Surgical masks do not filter PM2.5. Both myths are on this site, with sources.”

Judge: “What if there is no station in my city?”

“Then we use the Open-Meteo CAMS model (45 km grid) and label it ‘model, not a measurement’ — and the map says which cities are model-only.”
